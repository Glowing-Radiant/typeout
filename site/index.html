    Exam patterns

    PatternTimeStandard
    SSC CHSL – English10 minutes35 words per minute (1,750 key depressions)
    SSC CHSL – Hindi10 minutes30 words per minute (1,500 key depressions)
    SSC CGL – DEST15 minutes2,000 key depressions

    One word is counted as five key depressions, including spaces. The numbers follow recent SSC notices, but rules change: always read the notice for your exam, and use the Custom pattern or the error-limit setting to match it.

    Exam simulation behaves like the SSC software: the passage is not highlighted and the test cannot be paused. Practice highlights the current word, marks wrong words as you go and lets you pause.

    How marking works

    When you finish, your text is lined up with the passage word by word. Each difference is a full or a half mistake.

    MistakeExampleCounts as
    Missed word“the cat sat” typed as “the sat”Full
    Extra word“the cat” typed as “the big cat”Full
    Wrong word or spelling“record” typed as “recrod”Full
    Spacing“in the” typed as “inthe”, or “note book” for “notebook”Half
    Capital letters“India” typed as “india”Half
    Punctuation“however,” typed as “however”Half
    Word order“very good” typed as “good very”Half
    • Errors = full mistakes + (half mistakes ÷ 2).
    • Error percentage = errors ÷ passage words you attempted × 100. Text after the last word you typed is not counted against you.
    • Gross speed = (key depressions ÷ 5) ÷ minutes.
    • Net speed = (key depressions ÷ 5 − errors) ÷ minutes.
    • KDPH = key depressions per hour.
    • A word cut off when the time runs out is not counted as a mistake.
    • You meet the standard when you reach the target key depressions in the time allowed and your error percentage is within the limit.

    Dictation

    Choose how Dictation reads the passage to you when you set up a test:

    • Device voice: your device's speech voice reads each part. Nothing is sent to your screen reader.
    • My screen reader: each part is sent to your screen reader, which reads it in its own voice and speed. The device voice stays silent. Turning off typed-character echo in your screen reader stops it talking over the dictation.
    • Off: nothing is read aloud.
    • Parts are one sentence at a time or a set number of words.
    • The next part is read once you have typed every word of the part before it, so a new part never starts while you are still on the last one. If you prefer no gap, turn on Read the next part before I finish the current one and choose how many words before the end it starts.
    • When you stop typing for the number of seconds you choose, the current part is repeated (up to three times), or the next part is read anyway so the dictation keeps a steady pace. You can also turn this off.
    • Listen only hides the passage, so you type from what you hear. Your text is still marked against the passage.
    • You can have punctuation spoken (“comma”, “full stop”), choose the device voice and its speed, and repeat the current part at any time with Alt+Shift+R.

    Device voices come from your device. For Hindi, you may need to add a Hindi voice in your system's speech settings, or use Chrome, which includes one. With your screen reader, Hindi parts are marked as Hindi so it can switch to a Hindi voice if you have one.

    Online passages and the scoreboard

    New passage each time takes a fresh passage from Wikipedia for every test, on subjects like the economy, the environment, science, history and public services, so you never run out of practice text. The text is cleaned up for typing: headings, references, lists, bracketed notes and most figures are removed, and curly quotes and long dashes become keys on an ordinary keyboard. Each passage names its article and the CC BY-SA 4.0 licence. Wikipedia articles are not written in the style of SSC passages, so expect more names and technical words.

    A couple of passages are fetched ahead of time and kept in this browser, so a test starts straight away and still works for a while without a connection. If none is available, a built-in passage is used instead.

    After an attempt on SSC CHSL English, SSC CHSL Hindi, SSC CGL DEST or the Quick drill, in either mode, you can share your score under a name you choose. Exam simulation and Practice have separate scoreboards, because Practice highlights the passage and lets you pause. To be shared, an attempt has to run until the time is up or reach the end of the passage, reach the target key depressions and stay within 10% errors. Your typed text is sent with the score so the server can mark it again with the same rules. The Quick drill has a scoreboard for each language. The scoreboard shows the 10 best names for each pattern and mode, all time or for the last 7 days.

    Typing in Hindi

    Hindi passages are in Unicode, the same text you see in Mangal or Nirmala UI fonts. Type them with a Hindi keyboard on your device:

    • Windows: Settings → Time & language → Language & region → Add a language → Hindi. Add the Hindi Traditional keyboard (InScript). Switch with Windows+Space.
    • Remington (GAIL): install a Remington GAIL Unicode keyboard, such as the free Indic input tools published for government use, and choose it from the language bar.
    • macOS: System Settings → Keyboard → Input Sources → add Hindi – InScript.
    • Android and iPhone: add Hindi to your on-screen keyboard. An external keyboard gives a far more realistic test.

    Practise on the layout you will choose in the exam.

    Accessibility

    Type Out aims to meet WCAG 2.2 level AA. It works with the keyboard alone and with screen readers such as NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver and TalkBack. You can change the theme, text size, spacing and typeface in Settings, and the reading aids let you hear the passage while you type.

    If something does not work for you, please tell us so we can fix it.
